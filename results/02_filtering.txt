BAND-PASS FILTERING 8-30 Hz — subject 1, run 4
==========================================================================

--- THE FILTER MNE ACTUALLY BUILDS ---
  band-pass          : 8-30 Hz
  type               : FIR, zero-phase (no time shift at all)
  length             : 265 coefficients = 1.66 s of signal
  -> every output sample is computed from 1.66 s of
     context around it. That is WHY we filter the continuous signal
     before cutting it into epochs, and never the other way round: a
     5 s epoch does not provide enough context, and its edges would
     be distorted.

--- WHERE IS THE POWER IN THE RAW SIGNAL? (mean over 64 channels) ---
  0.5-4 Hz   (drift, delta)         65.8 %  #################################
  4-8 Hz     (theta)                12.4 %  ######
  8-12 Hz    (mu)     <- useful      5.8 %  ###
  13-30 Hz   (beta)   <- useful      9.4 %  #####
  30-79 Hz   (EMG, mains)            5.1 %  ###

  -> useful band (8-30 Hz) : 15.2 % of total power
  -> below 8 Hz            : 78.2 %
  This is THE number that justifies filtering: without it, the variance
  CSP tries to maximise would be dominated by slow drift, and it would
  optimise on noise instead of motor activity.

--- SANITY CHECK: DOES THE FILTER DO WHAT WE ASKED? ---
    2.0 Hz (rejected):   -49.8 dB
    5.0 Hz (rejected):   -50.0 dB
   10.0 Hz (    kept):    +0.0 dB
   20.0 Hz (    kept):    -0.0 dB
   40.0 Hz (rejected):   -46.0 dB
   60.0 Hz (rejected):   -54.9 dB
  (0 dB = untouched ; -30 dB = power divided by 1000)
  The mains spike at 60 Hz, clearly visible in step 2, is gone.

--- SIGNAL AMPLITUDE, BEFORE AND AFTER ---
  raw      :  53.5 uV (std, all channels)
  filtered :  20.1 uV
  -> amplitude divided by 2.7.
     This is NOT a loss of useful signal: it is the disappearance of
     the slow drift that was inflating amplitude without carrying any
     information about motor intent.
